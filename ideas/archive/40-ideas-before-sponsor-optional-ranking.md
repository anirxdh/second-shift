# 40 Multi-App AI Agent Hackathon Ideas — Ambitious Product Visions

Updated September 13, 2026. This collection incorporates the user's preference for ambitious, creative, useful products and one compelling end-to-end happy-path demonstration. No idea is selected and no implementation is approved.

## Current direction

Think beyond a small utility. Each concept now has a broader product vision, a specific complete demo journey, and a reason for voice or 3D. The demo journey is the first implemented experience within that vision, not a ceiling on what the product can become. The forty original identities and numbering are retained, with **#31 Tomorrow Museum expanded into Tomorrow**.

A curated happy path is the presentation strategy. Show real connected-app actions, obtain the approvals the chosen workflow requires, and verify the final records. Label simulated app state and hypothetical scenarios honestly. Do not imply that the full product vision or untested recovery paths are already implemented.

The brief: **Build one useful, multi-step AI agent. Connect it to at least three external apps. Show how you know it works.**

| Criterion | Weight | How we address it |
|---|---:|---|
| Technical execution | 30% | A connected workflow with actual reads, decisions, and writes |
| Reliability and evaluation | 25% | Recorded tool results, final-state checks, and repeated runs of the implemented scenario |
| Usefulness | 20% | A specific user's real task reaches a useful outcome |
| Originality | 15% | An unusual mechanism or interaction that contributes to that outcome |
| Demo clarity | 10% | A visible problem, understandable decisions, and a verified result |

For choosing the product, usefulness remains our first priority. Three.js is our interface library, not an external app. ElevenLabs can supply the voice interface; each demo should still meaningfully connect at least three operational apps. Arga supplies supported service simulations and Lemma can capture our backend's execution evidence. A simulation does not send real email, transfer money, or prove access to the real provider.

Famous people below are creative lenses, not the authors or endorsers of these ideas. Working names, competitive differentiation, user demand, and exact integrations are not validated. Assume hackathon access covers the lowest paid tier we need; actual limits remain unconfirmed.

## Ideas 1–30 — Expanded product directions

### 1. Unblock

**Creative lens:** **Elon Musk — remove the bottleneck**

**Original problem:** **Unblock:** A small workshop cannot complete an order because one part or resource is missing.

**Ambitious product vision:** A production control room that connects customer orders, parts, suppliers, staffing, and delivery commitments across a workshop or factory.

**Connected app responsibilities:** Reads orders and stock in **Sheets**, finds supplier correspondence in **Gmail**, proposes a revised **Calendar** schedule, and assigns recovery tasks in **Slack**.

**Primary happy-path demo:** A supplier delay threatens an order. Read stock in Sheets, find an approved supplier alternative in Gmail, revise the production booking in Calendar, and publish the recovery assignment in Slack.

**Voice and 3D:** A 3D assembly line highlights the missing dependency; the operator asks by voice how to keep the delivery promise.

### 2. ChangeOrder

**Creative lens:** **Garry Tan — solve something someone will pay for**

**Original problem:** **ChangeOrder:** Freelancers lose money when “one small change” becomes unpaid work.

**Ambitious product vision:** A commercial operating layer for creative agencies: track scope, detect changes, assemble approved proposals, and keep delivery and billing aligned.

**Connected app responsibilities:** Compares a new **Gmail** request against the agreed scope in **Docs**, drafts a priced change proposal, and creates a **Stripe** invoice after approval.

**Primary happy-path demo:** A client requests three extra deliverables. Compare Gmail against the signed scope in Docs, draft the change proposal, obtain approval, and create the matching Stripe invoice with a linked delivery checklist.

**Voice and 3D:** A project grows visually as scope is added; voice explains exactly which requested work sits outside the existing agreement.

## Ideas 31–40 — Playful concepts, expanded

The original detailed workflows, proof questions, and uncertainties remain below. The new product vision and primary happy path make their ambition explicit. Existing starting fixtures illustrate a demonstration, not a product-size limit. Additional edge cases are a future evaluation backlog unless included in the implemented demo.

### 31. Tomorrow — a playable business model (originally Tomorrow Museum)

**Ambitious product vision:** Tomorrow: a playable model of an organization's operational commitments. Rehearse decisions and disruptions, inspect consequences, and execute an approved recovery across apps.

**Primary happy-path demo:** Tour an upcoming event using Calendar, Sheets, and Gmail; simulate a supplier cancellation in an isolated scenario, find an available backup, produce the Docs recovery plan, then reserve and notify after approval.

**Expanded voice and 3D direction:** A narrated 3D world has visibly distinct current, hypothetical, proposed, and confirmed states. The experience is a scenario rehearsal, not a forecast.

**Unexpected angle:** Instead of asking an agent what to do tomorrow, ask it to show you a plausible exhibit of how tomorrow could go wrong—and then prevent that specific failure.

**Who needs it:** A small event or workshop organizer with a plan scattered across apps.

**Opening line:** “Give me a tour of tomorrow's workshop as if our projector supplier cancelled.”

**Workflow:** Read the room and session schedule from **Calendar**, equipment requirements and backup stock from **Sheets**, and confirmed supplier commitments from **Gmail**. Apply an explicitly selected hypothetical failure to a copy of that state. Identify the affected session, search the approved backup inventory, and draft a recovery plan in **Docs**. After approval, reserve the alternative in Calendar and notify the designated staff through Gmail.

**Voice and 3D:** Walk through a tiny museum of the hypothetical failure: an empty projector stand, a delayed session, and the backup that fixes it. ElevenLabs narrates each finding with its evidence. Toggle between “current plan,” “hypothetical failure,” and “approved repaired plan.” This is an inspectable scenario—not a prediction of the future.

**Verification targets and future evaluation cases:** Hypothetical changes must never leak into real records. Every affected session must follow an explicit dependency. The proposed backup must be available and satisfy the declared requirements; no valid backup means the agent asks for help.

**Example starting fixtures (not the product ceiling):** One event, one failure type, one dependency chain, one repair. **Main uncertainty:** whether organizers value rehearsal enough to use it before a crisis.

### 32. Meeting Funeral — an agent that retires a meeting and keeps its useful work alive

**Ambitious product vision:** A work-retirement agent that identifies the obligations behind recurring coordination and helps teams replace selected meetings with complete asynchronous workflows.

**Primary happy-path demo:** Read the chosen Calendar meeting, collect Linear status and Slack context, publish an approved replacement update with decision owners, and cancel that occurrence after organizer approval.

**Expanded voice and 3D direction:** Retire the meeting with a playful ceremony while visibly preserving every remaining obligation; show scheduled time released without inventing productivity gains.

**Unexpected angle:** A productivity agent whose successful output is one less recurring task.

**Who needs it:** A small team holding a weekly status meeting that may be replaceable with an asynchronous update.

**Opening line:** “Can we retire Tuesday's status meeting without losing its decisions or leaving anyone blocked?”

**Workflow:** Read the meeting's stated purpose and agenda in **Calendar**, inspect the relevant **Linear** project, and collect updates from a specified **Slack** channel. Draft the replacement status update in Slack, name unanswered decisions, and propose an owner for each. If the organizer approves and unresolved decisions are accounted for, cancel only the selected meeting occurrence and publish the approved update. Keep genuinely necessary decision time.

**Voice and 3D:** A small ceremony places the retired meeting on a shelf while its obligations move into visible owner/task cards. A clock shows the scheduled attendee-time released, explicitly labeled as scheduled time—not measured productivity gains. The voice explains what survived the cancellation.

**Verification targets and future evaluation cases:** Never cancel solely because an AI calls a meeting useless. ## Worked example — Loadout’s complete demo journey

The problem statement:

> When equipment goes missing before an event, small production crews must rebuild a workable setup across inventory, bookings, suppliers, and staff messages. Loadout turns that disruption into a verified recovery plan.

A concrete demo: an event starts in 90 minutes. Its planned PA speaker is unavailable.

You say:

> “We’re missing the main speaker for tonight. Find a workable replacement under the approved budget. Don’t move equipment already promised to another event.”

The agent:

1. Reads inventory and equipment requirements.
2. Checks which alternatives are actually available during the event.
3. Rejects an option already reserved elsewhere.
4. Checks an explicitly modeled compatibility requirement.
5. Presents a valid replacement and its costs.
6. After approval, reserves it and assigns the crew.
7. Verifies the reservation before saying the equipment is secured.

**The Three.js interface:** A small stage with equipment positions and a nearby inventory area. Click the missing speaker to see the affected setup. The proposed replacement appears as a preview; it becomes confirmed only after the reservation succeeds.

**The ElevenLabs interface:** A crew member can explain the problem and answer follow-up questions without typing. We use our own chosen assistant voice—not a celebrity imitation.

**The apps:** Sheets for equipment data, Calendar for reservations, Gmail for supplier correspondence, and Slack for crew assignments. The agent must perform meaningful work across at least three of them.

**Further evaluation opportunities:** Seed cases where the cheapest option is booked, the available option is incompatible, no valid option exists, or a reservation fails. Check the final app state against explicit expected outcomes. Arga can host supported app twins; Lemma can record our decisions and tool results.

The most memorable moment could be:

> “That replacement looks available—but it belongs to tomorrow’s morning setup. I found another option that won’t break the next event.”

That demonstrates reasoning people can understand immediately.

A first demo can use one equipment category and two events inside the broader production world. Compatibility comes from explicit fixture data. This fixture is a starting journey; it does not limit the product vision to a small inventory utility.

## Happy-path demonstration and evidence

1. Choose one useful end-to-end journey through the ambitious vision. Make it complete across at least three external operational apps.
2. Prepare realistic starting records and any explicitly simulated participant responses. Describe the environment as simulated when it is; do not imply a real customer accepted a fixture response.
3. Let the agent read the records and use working tools. The 3D scene should display the resulting state, with proposed and confirmed actions visibly distinct.
4. Preserve the workflow's real approval and acceptance steps as part of the successful journey.
5. Verify final records against explicit expectations: correct IDs, fields, destinations, quantities, and statuses. Save evidence links and tool results.
6. Repeat the implemented scenario from a clean starting state. Report only observed runs and results. Use Lemma for the backend execution trail where configured; use direct state assertions for proof.
7. Present a cinematic two-minute story: real problem, spoken request, understandable agent decisions, visible completed outcome, and a brief evidence view.
8. Keep optional fault injection and broader edge-case coverage as a later evaluation backlog. A happy-path demo does not establish production readiness or unimplemented functionality.
